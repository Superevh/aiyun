The best virtual team building game is one that starts in under a minute, works for everyone on the call and ends before people want it to. For most teams that means a short browser game: send one link, people join from a laptop or phone, and nobody creates an account.

This guide covers ten free games from RetroLift, which ones suit your group size, and three session plans you can run as written.

Which game suits your group size

Group size matters more than anything else. Games where people answer at the same time hold up with 40 players, while games built around one person's story work best when everyone gets a turn.

Three session plans

Pick by the time you have. Start with something everyone can do without thinking, put the most active game in the middle, and stop while people are still enjoying it.

10 minutes, at the end of a regular meeting

  1. 0–5 min: Hot Take Slider. Ask the people at either end of the slider to explain themselves.
  2. 5–10 min: Higher or Lower.

30 minutes, for a Friday wind-down

  1. 0–5 min: Emoji Charades while latecomers arrive.
  2. 5–25 min: Draw & Guess on a shared screen, with people guessing from their phones.
  3. 25–30 min: Zoom In as a quick closer.

60 minutes, for a new team or a kick-off

  1. 0–5 min: Hot Take Slider as a warm-up.
  2. 5–20 min: Two Truths and a Lie, so people learn something about each other.
  3. 20–35 min: Whose Photo? Give a theme such as "your current desk".
  4. 35–55 min: Draw & Guess for the noisy part of the session.
  5. 55–60 min: Mind Meld, which the team wins or loses together.

Running one without it feeling forced

  • Test the link first. Open the room a few minutes early and join from your phone, so the first minute of the session isn't spent troubleshooting.
  • Share your screen. A shared view keeps everyone watching the same thing, even if some people are only half on camera.
  • Keep cameras optional. All of these games can be played with the camera off.
  • Think about language. Zoom In and Emoji Charades lean on pictures, which is fairer than word-heavy games when the team has mixed first languages.
  • Plan for the host dropping out. Draw & Guess has an "Anyone can run the game" switch, so one person's Wi-Fi doesn't end the round.
  • Hold it in working hours. Team building done in people's personal time rarely feels like a treat. Rotate the slot if your team spans time zones.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best free virtual team building games?

Draw & Guess is the liveliest, Two Truths and a Lie is the best for getting to know people, and Hot Take Slider is the quickest to start. All three are free in RetroLift and need no sign-up.

What is a good virtual team building game for a large group?

Choose a game where everyone answers at once. Hot Take Slider, Higher or Lower, Emoji Charades and Zoom In all take up to 40 players, and nobody waits for a turn.

Do these games work with Zoom or Microsoft Teams?

Yes. They run in a browser alongside any video call. The host shares their screen and players join from the link or QR code on a laptop or phone.

How long should a virtual team building session be?

Ten minutes at the end of a meeting is often enough. Keep a dedicated social to 30 to 60 minutes and finish on time.
