How to get traffic to a new website with zero budget: what worked in our first 10 days (real data)

Real numbers from launching three small sites with no ad budget: which channels brought visitors, which brought none, and what we would do first next time.

By Evan, AIYUN · Updated 2026-11-15

In late October 2026 we launched three small websites with no ad budget: RetroLift, a free retrospective board; Lift Apps, a family of agile tools; and this site. We tried every free channel we could reach, logged every action, and counted visits with our own cookie-free counter. This page is the honest scorecard after the first ten days, including the channels that brought nobody.

The short version: launch-day sharing and Product Hunt bring a spike that fades within days. Search is the only channel that kept growing, but only after we fixed indexing. Most content platforms bring nothing to a new account.

What we learned

1. The launch spike is not traction

Day two had 174 views; day eight had 6. Almost all of the spike came from people we shared links with and from Product Hunt. Plan for the drop, and do not judge a channel by launch week.

2. Search starts with indexing, not ranking

We submitted a sitemap and assumed Google would read it. It had read only 2 of our pages after three days. Resubmitting the sitemap and asking Search Console to index pages one by one took Google from 2 known pages to 124. Only then did impressions start to climb. If a new site gets no search traffic, check how many pages Google actually knows about before writing more content.

3. Write for phrases people type, not phrases you like

Bing's free keyword tool showed real monthly searches for phrases like "question of the day for work" and "this or that questions for work". Pages retitled around those phrases are the ones now getting impressions. Pages titled the way we described our own product get none.

4. Content platforms do not lend you an audience

Posts on dev.to, Medium and Hashnode from new accounts got about 30 reads or fewer each and brought 3 visits in total. Their only value was a link back to our sites, which helps search engines find us.

5. Measure carefully, or the numbers will flatter you

For two days our counter treated Google's inspection tool as a new visitor every time we asked it to index a page. Filter crawlers, exclude your own visits, and count only page loads where someone scrolled or tapped. Our "engaged" count is about a third of raw page views.

6. The bottleneck is links from other sites

Bing reports only two other sites linking to RetroLift. Search engines rank pages partly by who links to them, and the communities that create those links (Reddit, Hacker News, niche forums) need a real person with a history there. No tool can do that part for you.

If we started again

  1. Set up a cookie-free counter and Search Console on day one, before any promotion.
  2. Launch on Product Hunt and share with people you know, but expect a spike, not a base.
  3. Spend most of the effort on pages that answer real search phrases, and check every week that they are indexed.
  4. Link every new page from a page that is already indexed.
  5. Post in one or two communities where you already have a history, as yourself.
  6. Skip content platforms unless you already have followers there.

Update, day 11 (8 November)

Total page views across the three sites reached 577, up from 544 at day ten. Two things stood out.

On quiet days, most raw views are bots

Overnight on 7 to 8 November every new page view came from automated crawlers: five or eight pages opened three seconds apart, no referrer, no scrolling. On 7 November, 13 raw views contained about one or two real people. At this scale raw counts can be mostly noise, which is why we judge channels by engaged visits only.

Update, day 12 (9 November)

Raw page views reached 590. Three things we learned this weekend.

Check the product has the feature before chasing a keyword

Bing's keyword tool showed about 2,700 searches in three months for "human bingo", the "find someone who" mixer. Our bingo game only had meeting bingo. Rewriting the page to rank for human bingo would have sent people to something they could not use, so we first added a real "find someone who" card set (36 prompts, a different card for each player, printable), which took about 30 minutes, and only then rewrote the page.

List searches want long lists, so we made the product's list longer

The top ten results for "hot take questions" are all lists of 80 to 200 items. Our hot takes page listed the 42 statements built into the game. We added 60 more to the game itself, including a new tech and agile pack, and the page now lists all 102 straight from the game, so the two can never disagree. About five hours later the page got its first visitor from Google who scrolled and tapped. One visit is an anecdote, not proof; we will compare impressions and rank on 14 November.

Some Search Console impressions are not people

Our Chinese meeting cost calculator showed 518 impressions at position 5.5 and no clicks. The queries were oddly specific ("hybrid ticketing cost calculation", "photography workshop fee calculation") and each had 20 to 29 impressions with nearly identical counts. That pattern looks like automated querying rather than real demand, so we now read impressions query by query before calling a page a success.

Update, day 17 (14 November): what worked and what did not

Raw page views across the three sites reached 673. On day 11 we promised to report the results of our tests on 14 November, including the ones that do not work. Search Console data runs to 10 November, so changes made after that are not in it yet.

Search, last 28 days

The retro and games site (fun-retro.com) had 14 clicks from 1,240 impressions at an average position of 28. This site (aiyun.com.au) had 2 clicks from 219 impressions. Of the 16 clicks, 8 went to game pages, 3 to lists of icebreaker questions, 2 to the fun-retro home page, 1 to a retrospective template and 2 to Chinese guides on this site.

The biggest multiplier was the product, not search

Twice this week a visitor found the Zoom In game on Google, opened a room and pasted the link into a Microsoft Teams meeting. Six people joined each time, so 2 search visitors brought 12 players. That is close to the 16 search clicks both sites had in four weeks. For a small multiplayer tool, making the room link easy to share may matter more than another landing page.

What we would tell another small site

  • In the first weeks, clicks come from pages where the product itself is the answer, such as a game someone can play right away.
  • Add to pages that already rank before writing new ones for big keywords.
  • A better title does not rescue a page whose impressions come from the wrong searches.

Update, day 18 (15 November): Google indexed 129 pages, Bing indexed 1

We checked how much of each site the two big search indexes hold. Google's Search Console shows 129 pages of fun-retro.com indexed and 8 not indexed. A Bing site: search on the same day returned 1 page for fun-retro.com (the home page), 6 for this site and 1 for liftapps.com.au.

For more than two weeks we had sent every new or changed page to Bing through IndexNow, and the technical checks were clean: robots.txt allows everything, the IndexNow key file loads, pages return 200 to Bing's crawler, and canonical tags are correct. Many inner pages are also linked from guides on this site that Bing has indexed.

  • Indexing is no longer the bottleneck on Google. Adding more pages and asking Google to index them again has stopped paying off. What limits clicks now is ranking and demand: about 1,100 impressions and 11 clicks a week.
  • IndexNow on its own did not get a new small site into Bing. Bing's index also feeds AI answers from Copilot, ChatGPT search and DuckDuckGo, so an AI assistant that searches through Bing can barely find these pages. Our next step is to submit the sitemap and key pages by hand in Bing Webmaster Tools.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take a new website to get search traffic?

In our case the first visitor from Google search arrived within the first week, and impressions grew about eight times between the first and second week. Clicks stay rare until pages reach the first page of results, which usually takes weeks.

Is Product Hunt worth it for a small free tool?

Once, yes. It brought 13 tracked visits in a few days and nothing after. The forums on the same site brought 2.

Do I need a cookie banner to count visitors?

Not necessarily. We count page views without cookies or IP addresses. Some privacy rules still treat local storage like cookies, so check what applies to your visitors.

Who ran this experiment?
